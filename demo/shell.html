Contactless Pulse Recorder

Camera-based pulse (rPPG) with a generic rhythm view

Demo · simulated signal

No camera is used here. A simulated face signal stands in for the webcam, and the app's real signal-processing code analyses it. Because the simulation knows the true heartbeats, this demo can show how well beat detection works, which the real app can't. Nothing on this page is a measurement of you. It's not an ECG and not for diagnosis.

How this demo works
      
    
        
  • The simulator builds a blood-volume pulse (systolic peak plus dicrotic wave) for each heartbeat and darkens a skin
          colour by it, mostly in green, the way haemoglobin absorption does. It adds lighting drift and sensor noise, and in
          the poor-signal scenario also motion jumps and light flicker.
    • 
        
  • Those per-frame red, green and blue averages go through the same code the app uses: resampling to 30 Hz, the POS
          projection, a 0.7–4 Hz band-pass, spectral heart rate with SNR, peak detection and HRV.
    • 
        
  • "True beat" markers come from the simulator. In the real app there is no ground truth. Against a reference
          device, rPPG HRV is known to be noisy: a perfectly regular simulated pulse shows 20–40 ms of apparent RMSSD
          once realistic noise is added.
    • 
        
  • The rhythm view is hidden whenever quality is poor, because beats found in noise are mostly wrong and a
          tidy ECG drawn on them would mislead.
    • 
        
  • Quality comes from how concentrated the pulse is at one frequency. An irregular rhythm spreads that out, so
          it scores "poor" even when beats are detected well. A beat-shape based quality score would fix this; the app
          doesn't have one yet.
